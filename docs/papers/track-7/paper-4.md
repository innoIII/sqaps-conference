الملخص

Cross-border crime evolves within a fast-moving communication environment shaped by digital platforms, multilingual audiences, false identities, and sophisticated methods of persuasion and recruitment. Yet security-awareness messages often remain textheavy or describe threats without translating them into specific protective actions. This study identifies the characteristics that make visual security communication clear, understandable, and actionable, and examines how graphic design can support community prevention of cross-border crime. A descriptive-analytical, designoriented approach was adopted. A purposive sample of six official campaigns and awareness resources produced by INTERPOL, the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime, Europol, and Oman’s National Committee to Combat Human Trafficking was comparatively analysed. Seven dimensions guided the analysis: visual salience, threat clarity, actionability, cultural and linguistic adaptability, cross-channel integration, ethical representation, and measurability.
The findings indicate that stronger design practices combine one central idea, a short visual sequence, a direct protective action, and a trusted institutional identity. A recurring limitation was the limited public availability of indicators measuring comprehension, recall, or behavioural response. Based on these findings, the study proposes the BASIRA framework for visual security communication. It comprises six connected stages: defining the risk and desired behaviour; understanding the audience and context; constructing visual codes and narratives; linking the message to an executable action; adaptive multi-channel deployment; and testing, measurement, and improvement. The study recommends developing a flexible visual security system, pre-testing symbols and messages with intended audiences, and evaluating performance beyond digital reach to include comprehension, action, and reporting.
Keywords: Visual communication; graphic design; security awareness; cross-border crime; community prevention; risk communication.

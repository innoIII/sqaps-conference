المستخلص

This study aimed to identify the role of security capabilities in reducing transnational crimes. The researcher employed a descriptive-analytical survey methodology, utilizing a questionnaire as the study instrument. A simple random sample of 743 individuals was selected. The study yielded several key findings, the most prominent of which are as follows: The current level of security capabilities utilized in reducing transnational crimes was rated very high, with a mean score of (4.27). The dimensions of security capabilities were ranked according to their level of utilization as follows: “Innovation and Digital Transformation” ranked first with a mean score of (4.39), followed by “International Cooperation” in second place with a mean score of (4.32), followed by “Policies and Legislation” in third place with a mean score of (4.27), followed by “Human Capabilities” in fourth place with a mean score of (4.22), followed by “Technical Capabilities” in fifth place with a mean score of (4.16). The degree of impact of utilizing security capabilities in reducing transnational crimes was found to be very high, with a mean score of (4.27). The level of challenges facing the utilization of security capabilities in reducing transnational crimes was rated high, with a mean score of (4.11). The most significant challenges identified include: the rapid evolution of transnational crime methods, discrepancies in systems and legislation across countries, inadequate exchange of information related to transnational crimes, and the difficulty of tracking transnational criminal activities. The study concluded with several recommendations, the most important of which are: training human capabilities in advanced intelligence analysis skills, prioritizing the use of artificial intelligence technologies in predicting crimes before they occur, emphasizing the enactment of flexible legislation coupled with international compliance, and establishing shared and secure platforms for the exchange of data and intelligence among security agencies locally and internationally, thereby contributing to improved speed and accuracy in decision-making.
Keywords: Security Capabilities, Security, Transnational Crimes, Crime.

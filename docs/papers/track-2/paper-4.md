Abstract

الملخص

This paper examines the legal protections afforded to victims of human trafficking in light of the provisions of the Palermo Protocol and the Omani Anti-Trafficking Law (promulgated by Royal Decree No. 78/2025). It analyzes the legal and procedural safeguards established by the legislator for victims, focusing specifically on the principle of non-punishment for acts directly linked to their victimization. Furthermore, the paper explores the potential for utilizing artificial intelligence technologies to enhance proactive protection and the early detection of human trafficking crimes, while outlining the associated legal and ethical challenges. It also examines the adequacy of existing legal frameworks to keep pace with rapid digital transformations, ultimately deriving a set of findings and recommendations aimed at enhancing protection mechanisms, safeguarding victims’ rights, and striking a balance between the requirements of criminal justice and the demands of prevention.
Keywords: Anti, Trafficking in Persons Law, Palermo Protocol, Non-Punishment of Victims

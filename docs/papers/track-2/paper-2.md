Abstract
This study aimed to examine the alignment of the Sultanate of Oman’s legal frameworks and institutional arrangements for security and judicial cooperation in combating transnational organized crime with international standards, by analyzing international and regional conventions, national legislation, and measuring the efficiency of their practical implementation. The study relied on the foundational approach to establish the theoretical framework, and the critical analytical approach to examine the Omani system. The study concluded that international cooperation in combating organized crime has evolved from optional cooperation into a binding legal and institutional system led by the Vienna, Palermo, and Anti-Corruption conventions. This system is supported by mechanisms such as mutual legal assistance, extradition, asset recovery, and the transfer of criminal proceedings, thereby achieving a balance between the effectiveness of criminal prosecution and respect for sovereignty and human rights. Furthermore, the findings demonstrated that Omani legislation achieved a high level of compliance with these obligations through the development of laws on combating money laundering, human trafficking, and cybercrimes, alongside strengthening bilateral, regional, and international cooperation. The study also indicated that the establishment of specialized national institutions— foremost among which are the National Center for Financial Information and the National Committee to Combat Human Trafficking—in addition to partnerships with INTERPOL and regional organizations, has contributed to enhancing the efficiency of information exchange, tracking criminal proceeds, and fostering judicial and security cooperation. The study recommended the continuous updating of national legislation to keep pace with digital and financial crimes, expanding bilateral and regional agreements, and establishing unified digital platforms for the secure and immediate exchange of intelligence, financial, and judicial information. Additionally, it recommended enhancing training programs and institutional cooperation with competent international and regional entities to support the effectiveness of the national system in countering transnational organized crime.
Keywords: Organized Crime; International Cooperation; Sultanate of Oman; Palermo Convention; INTERPOL; Mutual Legal Assistance.

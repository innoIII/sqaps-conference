Abstract

مستخلص البحث

Rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies manipulation has fundamentally transformed strategies for combating transnational crimes, shifting criminal justice systems from reactive enforcement toward proactive and predictive security models. This research examines artificial intelligence as a proactive tool in combating transnational crime, while critically analyzing the legal and philosophical challenges arising from algorithmic decision-making and its implications for criminal justice guarantees and fundamental rights and freedoms.
The significance of this study lies in addressing a contemporary issue situated at the intersection of security, technology, and law. It seeks to reassess and/or re-evaluate the concept of criminal security in light of the transition from traditional reactive approaches —based on post-crime investigation— to predictive models relying on big data analytics and behavioral forecasting. The research aims to explore whether AI-driven security mechanisms can enhance law enforcement effectiveness without undermining the principles of legality, due process, and fair trial guarantees.
The crucial research problem concerns how to achieve a balanced framework that reconciles proactive security requirements with the protection of individual rights amid increasing reliance on algorithmic systems. The study implements an analytical and comparative methodology, examining international legal trends, scholarly debates, and practical applications of AI in criminal justice contexts.
Eventually conclusions demonstrate that AI significantly strengthens the capacity to detect and prevent transnational criminal activities; however, unregulated reliance on algorithmic systems may generate risks related to privacy infringement, algorithmic bias, and reduced judicial oversight. The study recommends establishing comprehensive legal frameworks governing proactive AI use, ensuring algorithmic transparency, and maintaining meaningful human oversight as a fundamental safeguard for balanced and human-centered criminal justice in the digital age.
